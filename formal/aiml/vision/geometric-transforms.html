Geometric Transforms

Move every pixel somewhere else with one small matrix: shift, rotate, scale, shear, or change the point of view.

Affine transformations

Affine transform: a linear mapping (plus a shift) that keeps points, straight lines and planes.

Preserved:

  • Parallelism: parallel lines stay parallel
  • Ratios of distances along a line
  • Barycentres (centres) of weighted points

Not preserved: angles; distances (except along parallel lines).

Examples: translation, rotation, scaling, shearing. Note the grid: its lines stay straight and parallel.

The affine transform matrix

2×3 matrix

$$M = \begin{bmatrix} a & b & t_x \\ c & d & t_y \end{bmatrix}$$

Transformation

$$\begin{bmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} t_x \\ t_y \end{bmatrix}$$
  • \(\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}\): the linear part: rotation, scale, shear
  • \(\begin{bmatrix} t_x \\ t_y \end{bmatrix}\): the translation

Perspective transform

Perspective simulates a 3-D viewing angle.

Properties

  • Straight lines stay straight
  • Parallel lines may converge (a vanishing point)
  • Preserves collinearity

Use cases

Document scanning, bird's-eye view, rectification.

Matrix

A 3×3 homography matrix.

The perspective transform matrix

3×3 homogeneous matrix

$$H = \begin{bmatrix} h_{11} & h_{12} & h_{13} \\ h_{21} & h_{22} & h_{23} \\ h_{31} & h_{32} & h_{33} \end{bmatrix}$$

Transformation

$$\begin{bmatrix} x' \\ y' \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{w} \begin{bmatrix} h_{11} & h_{12} & h_{13} \\ h_{21} & h_{22} & h_{23} \\ h_{31} & h_{32} & h_{33} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

\(w = h_{31}x + h_{32}y + h_{33}\): dividing by it is what makes far things smaller.

You need 4 point pairs to solve for it (8 unknowns, since \(h_{33} = 1\)).

Affine vs perspective

Affine

  • 2×3 matrix (6 parameters)
  • Needs 3 point pairs
  • Preserves parallelism
  • Faster to compute

Perspective

  • 3×3 matrix (8 parameters)
  • Needs 4 point pairs
  • Allows vanishing points
  • More general

Rule: use affine if parallel lines stay parallel; perspective otherwise.

Shearing

Shear slants the shape of the image.

Horizontal

$$M = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & s_x & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Vertical

$$M = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ s_y & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Parallelism, ratios of distances, straight lines.

What does perspective NOT preserve?

Parallelism: parallel lines can converge.

When do you use affine vs perspective?

Affine if parallelism is kept; perspective for 3-D effects.

What is shearing?

A slanting transformation.

What size is the perspective matrix?

A 3×3 homography (8 free parameters).
