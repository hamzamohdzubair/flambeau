Linear, Affine, Conical, Convex

Four ways to combine vectors \(a\mathbf{u} + b\mathbf{v}\): the only difference is which coefficients you allow.

Linear, affine, conical, convex combinations

A combination of vectors is \( a_1\mathbf{x}_1 + a_2\mathbf{x}_2 + \dots + a_n\mathbf{x}_n \).

The same words for maps

Linear map \( f(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x} \)
Grid lines stay straight and evenly spaced; the origin stays put.

Affine map \( f(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b} \)
A linear map followed by a shift: the origin moves.

Affine maps keep affine combinations: the midpoint of a segment still lands on the midpoint.
